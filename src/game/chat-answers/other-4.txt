Part 1: Holding and tuning

Topic: Posture and hand position
Practise: Sit with the guitar on your right leg and keep your left wrist straight for ten minutes
Which part of the finger should press the string? = The fingertip, just behind the fret | The flat of the finger | The thumb | The knuckle
Where should your thumb rest when you fret a chord? = Behind the neck, opposite the fingers | On top of the neck | On the sixth string | Beside the nut
What should you do if your wrist aches after practice? = Lower the guitar and straighten the wrist | Grip the neck harder | Play faster | Press the strings harder
How do you keep a straight back while seated? = Sit upright with the guitar on your leg | Lean over the body | Lean back in the chair | Hold it at arm's length

Topic: Tuning by ear
Practise: Tune all six strings with a tuner, then check each against the fifth fret of the string below
What are the standard open strings from thickest to thinnest? = E A D G B E | E B G D A E | A D G C E A | D A D G B E
Which fret on the low E string matches the open A string? = The fifth fret | The fourth fret | The seventh fret | The twelfth fret
Which string is tuned to the fourth fret of the string below it? = The B string | The G string | The D string | The high E string
What do you do if a string sounds flat? = Tighten the tuning peg slowly | Loosen the peg | Press the string harder | Replace the string

Part 2: First chords

Topic: Open chords
Practise: Play E minor and A minor slowly, one strum each, until every string rings clearly. Then switch between them ten times without stopping. Next try the same with C major and G major. Do this for five minutes a day for a week and note which fingers feel awkward. Keep the pulse steady and do not stop to fix mistakes; just carry on to the next chord.
Which chord uses only two fingers, on the A and D strings? = E minor | C major | G major | D major
What do you do when a string buzzes in a chord? = Move the fingertip closer to the fret | Press with the flat of the finger | Strum harder | Loosen the string
Which string should you not strum in an open A chord? = The low E string | The high E string | The B string | The A string
How do you practise a chord change? = Switch slowly and keep the beat | Switch fast and ignore the beat | Stop and watch your hand each time

Topic: Strumming patterns
Practise: Count one-and-two-and-three-and-four-and while strumming down on the numbers and up on the "and"
Which stroke falls on the numbers in basic eighth-note strumming? = Downstrokes | Upstrokes | Muted strokes | Slides
What do you do if your strumming hand stops mid-bar? = Keep it moving and miss the strings | Stop and start again | Speed up | Switch hands
How do you keep time while learning a pattern? = Tap your foot and count aloud | Play it as fast as possible | Watch your fingers only | Skip the upstrokes
Which tool helps you hold a steady tempo? = A metronome | A capo | A tuner | A pick

Part 3: Playing a song

Topic: Chord changes in a song
Practise: Pick one song with three chords and play it through at half speed
What should you do before playing a song at full speed? = Play it slowly until the changes are clean | Learn the lyrics only | Skip the hard bars | Play only the chorus
Why do you look ahead to the next chord? = To move the fingers before the beat | To rest your wrist | To tune the guitar | To check the lyrics
What do you do with a chord change that keeps failing? = Drill just those two chords on their own | Skip the song | Play it louder | Change the key
How do you mark where each change falls? = Write the chord names above the lyrics | Memorise the whole song | Record the song | Count the words

Topic: Playing along with a recording
Practise: Play along with a slow recording and aim to stay on the beat, not to play every note
How do you find the tempo of a recording? = Tap along and count the beats | Guess by the singer | Measure the length | Check the key
What should you do when you lose your place? = Rejoin at the next bar line | Stop the recording | Start over from the beginning | Play faster
What helps you rejoin after a mistake? = Keep strumming and listen for the downbeat | Stop and fix the mistake | Look at the next chord only | Switch off the recording
Which part of the song is best to start with? = The chorus, because it repeats | The bridge | The solo | The last verse

Keep practising a little every day and you will see progress!
