Sure! Here is your study list for GCSE Physics:

Part 1: Forces and Motion

Topic 1.1: Speed and velocity
Read - the chapter on scalars and vectors in your revision guide
Q: What is the difference between speed and velocity?
A: Velocity has a direction and speed does not
Wrong: Speed is measured in newtons, Velocity is always smaller than speed, Speed has a direction and velocity does not
Q: Which equation gives the average speed of an object?
A: Distance divided by time
Wrong: Time divided by distance, Mass times acceleration, Force divided by area
Q: What is the SI unit of velocity?
A: Metres per second
Wrong: Metres per second squared, Newtons, Joules per second

Topic 1.2: Newton's laws
Read - the three laws of motion and the worked examples on resultant force
Q: What does Newton's first law say about an object with no resultant force?
A: It stays at rest or keeps moving at a constant velocity
Wrong: It always slows down, It speeds up steadily, It moves in a circle
Q: Which equation links force, mass and acceleration?
A: F = ma
Wrong: F = mv, F = m/a, F = a/m
Q: What is the SI unit of force?
A: Newton
Wrong: Joule, Watt, Pascal

Part 2: Energy

Topic 2.1: Kinetic and potential energy
Read - the formulae for kinetic and gravitational potential energy
Q: Which equation gives kinetic energy?
A: Half times mass times speed squared
Wrong: Mass times gravity times height, Force times distance, Mass times speed
Q: What does gravitational potential energy depend on?
A: Mass, height and gravitational field strength
Wrong: Mass and speed only, Height and time only, Speed and gravitational field strength
Q: What energy store increases when you lift a book onto a shelf?
A: Gravitational potential
Wrong: Kinetic, Thermal, Elastic potential

Topic 2.2: Conservation of energy
Read - the idea that energy is transferred but never created or destroyed
Q: What does the law of conservation of energy say?
A: Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred
Wrong: Energy is used up when it is transferred, Energy is created in a battery, Energy always ends up as sound
Q: Where does most wasted energy usually end up?
A: As thermal energy in the surroundings
Wrong: As chemical energy, As gravitational energy, As nuclear energy
Q: How do you calculate efficiency?
A: Useful output energy divided by total input energy
Wrong: Total input energy divided by useful output energy, Useful output power times time, Total input energy minus useful output energy
