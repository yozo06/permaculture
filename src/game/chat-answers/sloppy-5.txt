Great choice! I have put together a study list for IELTS Speaking:

Part: Speaking Part 1
Topic: Answering personal questions
Read: Practise giving a direct answer and then one extra detail
What is the best way to answer "Do you work or study?" = Answer directly, then add a detail | Say only yes or no | Repeat the question back | Talk about something unrelated
Which reply sounds most natural to "Where are you from?" = I'm from Leeds, a city in the north of England | Leeds | From Leeds is where I am | I am a Leeds person from
How long should an answer in Part 1 usually be? = Two or three sentences | One word | A full minute | Five minutes
What should you do if you do not understand "Could you describe your hometown?" = Ask the examiner to repeat or explain | Stay silent | Change the subject | Answer in your own language
Topic: Common Part 1 themes
Read: Review the usual themes: home, work, study, hobbies, food and travel
Which of these is a typical Part 1 theme? = Hobbies | Politics of other countries | Quantum physics | Legal contracts
How many topics does the examiner usually cover in Part 1? = Two or three | Ten | One | Twenty
Is it acceptable to give a personal opinion in Part 1? = Yes, answers are about you | No, only facts | Only if the examiner asks | Only in Part 3

Part: Speaking Part 2
Topic: The long turn
Read: Practise speaking for two minutes from a cue card
How long do you speak in Part 2? = One to two minutes | Thirty seconds | Five minutes | Ten minutes
How long do you get to prepare before the long turn? = One minute | Five minutes | No time | Ten minutes
What is the cue card for? = It gives the topic and points to cover | It lists the answers | It tests your handwriting | It holds your score
Topic: Structuring the talk
Read: Use the points on the card as a simple plan: intro, details, feeling
What is a good way to organise a two-minute talk? = Follow the points on the cue card in order | Memorise a full script | Talk about everything you know | Skip the introduction
What should you do if you run out of ideas early? = Add a detail, an example or a feeling | Stop talking | Ask the examiner a question | Repeat your first sentence
Topic: Describing past experiences
Read: Revise the past simple and past continuous for stories

Part: Speaking Part 3
Topic: Discussing abstract ideas
Read: Practise giving opinions with reasons and examples
Which phrase is useful for giving an opinion? = In my view | Because of the | At the moment of | On the other of
How do you extend an answer in Part 3? = Give a reason and an example | Answer with one word | Ask for a new question | Read the cue card again
What is the examiner looking for in Part 3? = Developed ideas and clear reasons | One-word answers | Memorised phrases | A very long single sentence
Which question is a Part 3 question? = Why do people in cities spend less time with family? / What is your name? / Where do you live? / Do you like tea?
Topic: Comparing and speculating
Read: Learn language for comparing past and present and talking about the future
Q1. Which phrase is used to compare two things? = Whereas | Meanwhile of | In case of | Because to
Q2. Which structure is best for speculating about the future? = It might well be that | It did be that | It is being that | It were that
Q3. What does "On the one hand ... on the other hand" help you do? = Show two sides of an issue | Show a list of facts | End a conversation | Ask a question
Q4. Does a perfect accent matter in the test? = No, clear speech matters / Yes, it decides the score / Only in Part 1 / Only in Part 2

Happy studying!
