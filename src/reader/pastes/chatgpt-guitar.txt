What holds the strings at the top? = Tuning pegs | Frets | Pickguard | Strap pins

Where does the sound come from on an acoustic guitar? = Sound hole | Headstock | Bridge pins | Fretboard
Topic: Holding the guitar
Practise: sitting comfortably and holding the guitar securely
How should you sit while playing? = Comfortably upright | Leaning far forward | Lying down | Twisting sideways
Where should the guitar body rest? = Against your torso | Behind your back | On your shoulder | Against your knee only
How relaxed should your shoulders be? = Relaxed and natural | Raised and tense | Pulled backward | Completely stiff
Where should your fretting hand sit? = Around the guitar neck | Behind the guitar body | On the headstock | Under the guitar
How should you hold the guitar neck? = Gently without squeezing | As tightly as possible | With a closed fist | Using only your thumb

What tool helps beginners tune a guitar? = Clip-on tuner | Metronome | Guitar pick | Capo
Which string is the thickest standard string? = Low E | High E | B | G
Which string is the thinnest standard string? = High E | Low E | A | D
What should you do if a string sounds flat? = Tighten it slightly | Loosen it more | Remove the string | Press harder
What should you do before practising? = Check the tuning | Change all strings | Adjust the strap | Replace the pick

Topic: Using a guitar pick
Practise: holding a pick and making gentle strokes
How should you hold a guitar pick? = Between thumb and index finger | Between two palms | Between three fingers | In a closed fist
How much pick should stick out? = A small pointed section | The entire pick | Half your hand | Almost none
How tightly should you grip the pick? = Firmly but relaxed | As tightly as possible | Very loosely | With your whole hand
Which pick movement is called a downstroke? = Moving toward the floor | Moving toward the ceiling | Moving sideways | Moving backward
Which movement is called an upstroke? = Moving toward the ceiling | Moving toward the floor | Moving sideways | Moving backward

Part: First playing skills
Topic: Playing open strings
Practise: plucking each string individually with steady strokes
What is an open string? = A string played without pressing | A string played behind the neck | A string held down | A string removed
How many strings does a standard guitar have? = Six | Four | Five | Seven
What should you do to avoid hitting other strings? = Aim carefully at one string | Strum as hard as possible | Close your eyes | Hold every string
How can you make a clean single note? = Pluck one string clearly | Strike several strings | Press the bridge | Shake the guitar
What should you listen for after plucking? = A clear ringing note | A buzzing sound | A loose tuning peg | A silent string

Topic: Pressing the strings
Practise: pressing individual strings behind frets and checking clear notes
Where should you place your finger? = Just behind the fret | Directly on the fret | Far behind the fret | On the guitar body
Which part of your finger should press the string? = Fingertip | Fingernail | Finger joint | Finger pad only
How firmly should you press a string? = Only enough for a clear note | As hard as possible | Very lightly always | With your fingernail
What causes many buzzing notes? = Poor finger placement | Correct tuning | Relaxed shoulders | A clean pick
How can you stop touching nearby strings? = Curl your fingertips | Flatten your whole hand | Squeeze the neck | Bend your wrist sharply

Topic: First guitar chords
Read: the meaning of chords and beginner chord diagrams
What is a guitar chord? = Several notes played together | One note played alone | A guitar tuning method | A type of pick
What does a zero mean on a chord diagram? = Play the string open | Do not play the string | Press the first fret | Tune the string
What does an X mean on a chord diagram? = Do not play that string | Play it open | Press the second fret | Play it louder
What do numbers on chord diagrams show? = Which fingers to use | How loud to play | How long to strum | Which strings to replace
Which chord is commonly taught first? = Em | F#m | C#m | Bm

Topic: Your first chords
Practise: changing between Em, Am and C slowly
Which fingers usually play the Em chord? = Middle and ring fingers | Thumb and index finger | Index and little finger | All four fingers
What should you check after forming a chord? = Every intended string rings clearly | The guitar body vibrates loudly | Your fingers feel painful | The pick stays still
How should you practise changing chords? = Slowly and repeatedly | As fast as possible | Only once | Without looking
What helps make chord changes smoother? = Moving fingers together | Pressing much harder | Holding your breath | Strumming faster
What should you do if a chord buzzes? = Adjust finger placement | Strum harder | Turn the volume up | Shake the neck

Topic: Basic strumming
Practise: slow downstrokes and upstrokes across chord strings
What is strumming? = Sweeping across several strings | Plucking one string only | Turning tuning pegs | Pressing individual frets
Which hand usually strums the guitar? = Picking hand | Fretting hand | Both feet | Neither hand
What is a downstroke? = A stroke toward the floor | A stroke toward the ceiling | A finger slide | A string bend
What helps keep strumming steady? = Even arm movements | Random hand movements | Holding the pick tightly | Changing chords constantly
How should a beginner start strumming? = Slowly with gentle strokes | Quickly with force | Only with upstrokes | Without holding a chord

What is a metronome used for? = Keeping steady time | Changing guitar chords | Tuning strings | Cleaning the fretboard
What should you do when practising with a metronome? = Match its steady pulse | Play faster every time | Ignore its clicks | Stop between every click
What does a slow tempo mean? = A slower beat | A louder sound | A higher note | A shorter string
How should you increase your playing speed? = Gradually when comfortable | Immediately as fast as possible | Without practising slowly | By strumming harder

What helps when learning a new song? = Practising small sections | Playing everything immediately | Ignoring difficult changes | Strumming without timing
What should you do when you make a mistake? = Keep going and try again | Stop practising forever | Tune every string | Change your guitar
